# Agent brief: JPL Roman/Euclid application materials for Nhat-Minh Nguyen

## Purpose
Prepare application documents for two JPL positions focused on Roman and Euclid cosmology, especially galaxy weak lensing and galaxy clustering:

- R5541, Scientist IV
- R5540, Scientist II

The direct JPL pages may appear filled depending on the mirror, but indexed/search copies and AAS/Physics Today listings still show the position language. Treat the user's statement that the roles are unfilled as operationally true.

## Job language to target
The postings emphasize:

- Deep technical foundation in some combination of cosmological simulations, modeling, and cosmological data interpretation.
- Proven expertise in weak lensing and/or galaxy clustering as primary probes for investigating dark matter and dark energy.
- Strong interpersonal skills and ability to thrive in a collaborative team environment.
- Excellent written and verbal communication.
- Commitment to diverse ideas/backgrounds and an inclusive research culture.

Roman and Euclid context:

- Roman and Euclid are complementary dark-energy / dark-sector missions.
- Both use weak lensing and galaxy clustering.
- Euclid provides wide-area coverage; Roman provides deeper/more precise observations over a smaller area.
- Their overlap is scientifically valuable for cross-calibration, validation, and joint inference.

## Core application thesis
The application should communicate:

> I develop field-level and forward-modeling methods that help galaxy surveys extract robust dark-sector information from weak lensing and clustering, with applications to cosmic growth, gravity, dark energy, and primordial physics.

For Scientist IV:

> At JPL, I would use this expertise to help lead Roman and Euclid joint-probe cosmology, with an emphasis on nonlinear modeling, cross-survey calibration, and robust dark-sector interpretation.

For Scientist II:

> At JPL, I would use this expertise to contribute to Roman and Euclid weak-lensing and clustering analyses, especially through nonlinear modeling, likelihood validation, and dark-sector interpretation.

## Emotional/professional communication principle
The user wants to communicate intense commitment without sounding desperate. Do **not** say or imply:

- "I just need one chance."
- "I have never had an interview."
- "Before I leave academia."
- "I would be grateful for any opportunity."
- "I know I can prove myself."
- "I am desperate to continue."

Translate that emotional content into professional language:

> This position is unusually well aligned with the research program I have been building, and I would approach it with focus, care, and mission-oriented commitment.

A strong sentence to use near the end of the cover letter:

> I would approach this role with the focus and commitment of someone who sees Roman and Euclid as the natural place for field-level and forward-modeling methods to become useful at mission scale.

## Scientific brand
The user's research brand should be coherent, not diffuse:

> Field-level inference for fundamental physics with galaxy surveys.

Expanded:

> I develop forward-modeling and field-level inference methods that extract maximal information from large-scale structure, enabling precision tests of growth, gravity, dark energy, and primordial physics with real galaxy surveys.

Avoid branding the user as only:

- a field-level inference methodologist;
- a growth-of-structure person;
- a modified-gravity person;
- a PFS/DESI/Roman/Euclid survey person;
- a beyond-two-point-statistics person.

The best framing is:

> One recognizable technical signature, deployed across multiple probes and science cases.
