# Cover letter outline: JPL Roman/Euclid Scientist IV and Scientist II

## Overall strategy
The cover letter should not sound like a generic faculty application. It should sound mission-oriented:

1. JPL/Roman/Euclid have a concrete scientific need.
2. Minh has a directly relevant technical and scientific background.
3. Minh can contribute both independent ideas and collaborative mission work.
4. Minh is deeply committed, but professionally composed.

Core message:

> I can help JPL turn Roman and Euclid weak-lensing and clustering data into robust cosmological constraints by combining field-level/forward modeling, nonlinear large-scale-structure theory, survey-systematics control, and dark-sector phenomenology.

## Cover letter structure

### Paragraph 1: Opening and fit
Scientist IV version:

> I am applying for the Scientist IV position in Roman and Euclid cosmology because my research program directly addresses the central challenge of these missions: extracting robust dark-sector information from weak lensing and galaxy clustering in the presence of nonlinear structure formation, survey systematics, and modeling uncertainty.

Then:

> I work at the interface of nonlinear large-scale structure, statistical inference, and survey cosmology. My technical signature is field-level and forward-modeling inference for galaxy surveys, and my science goal is to use cosmic structure to test growth, gravity, dark energy, and primordial physics.

Scientist II version:

> I am applying for the Scientist II position because my background in large-scale-structure modeling, field-level Bayesian inference, and cosmological data interpretation is well aligned with JPL's Roman and Euclid science goals in weak lensing, galaxy clustering, and dark-sector cosmology.

Then:

> I would bring expertise in forward modeling, nonlinear structure formation, galaxy clustering, and growth-of-structure tests, together with a strong interest in helping Roman and Euclid deliver robust cosmological constraints.

### Paragraph 2: Roman/Euclid mission context
Purpose: show that Minh understands the mission, not just his own methods.

Suggested text:

> Roman and Euclid are entering a regime where the limiting factor will not simply be statistical power, but the ability to model nonlinear structure, galaxy bias, intrinsic alignments, photometric redshifts, survey selection, and cross-probe covariance with sufficient control. This is precisely where my research has focused: building inference frameworks that connect physically motivated forward models to cosmological interpretation. I am particularly interested in the Roman-Euclid overlap, where Euclid's wide-area coverage and Roman's depth and precision can be used not only for combined constraints, but also for calibration, validation, and systematic-error control.

### Paragraph 3: Technical signature
Suggested text:

> My core contribution is the development of field-level and forward-modeling methods for large-scale-structure cosmology. Rather than compressing galaxy surveys only into standard two-point summaries, my work asks how much additional information can be extracted from the cosmic web when the data are modeled at the level of fields, simulations, and physically controlled likelihoods. This expertise is directly relevant to Roman and Euclid, where weak lensing and galaxy clustering will require increasingly sophisticated modeling of nonlinear matter clustering, galaxy bias, intrinsic alignments, covariance, and survey realism.

### Paragraph 4: Landmark results
Include two landmarks:

1. Late-time suppression in growth of large-scale structure.
2. Field-level Bayesian inference framework for galaxy surveys, recognized by the 2024 Buchalter Cosmology Prize.

Suggested text:

> My previous work has produced two results especially relevant to this position. First, I contributed to evidence for a late-time suppression in the growth of large-scale structure, highlighting the importance of precise and internally consistent growth measurements for dark-energy and modified-gravity tests. Second, I developed field-level Bayesian inference methods for galaxy surveys, work recognized by the 2024 Buchalter Cosmology Prize. Together, these efforts reflect the same broader goal: using advanced modeling and inference to turn large-scale-structure data into sharper tests of the dark sector.

Scientist IV add-on:

> These projects required not only technical modeling, but also coordination across theory, simulations, statistical inference, and observational interpretation.

Scientist II add-on:

> These projects gave me direct experience translating theoretical and statistical ideas into analysis frameworks relevant to survey data.

### Paragraph 5: Specific JPL contributions
Four contribution areas:

1. Joint weak-lensing and galaxy-clustering inference.
2. Nonlinear and simulation-based validation of cosmological likelihoods.
3. Roman-Euclid cross-calibration and consistency tests.
4. Dark-sector interpretation: growth, gravity, dark energy.

Suggested text:

> At JPL, I would aim to contribute in four concrete areas: joint weak-lensing and galaxy-clustering inference; nonlinear and simulation-based validation of cosmological likelihoods; Roman-Euclid cross-calibration and consistency tests; and dark-sector interpretation in terms of growth, gravity, and dark energy. My field-level background is useful here not because every Roman or Euclid analysis should become fully field-level immediately, but because it provides a rigorous way to identify information loss, validate summary statistics, test modeling assumptions, and design analyses that remain robust as statistical errors shrink.

### Paragraph 6: Collaboration and team science
Suggested text:

> I have worked across theory, simulation, and observational-cosmology communities, and I value collaborative science where methods are judged by their ability to improve shared analyses rather than by technical novelty alone. Roman and Euclid cosmology will require sustained coordination among instrument teams, survey scientists, pipeline developers, theorists, statisticians, and external science users. I would bring a collaborative, transparent working style, with an emphasis on reproducible analysis, clear documentation, and mentorship of junior researchers.

Scientist IV add-on:

> I would also be prepared to help define analysis priorities, coordinate cross-team efforts, and represent JPL scientifically within Roman, Euclid, and broader dark-energy survey communities.

Scientist II add-on:

> I would be ready to contribute to existing analysis efforts while developing a focused independent role within the Roman/Euclid cosmology program.

### Paragraph 7: Closing with commitment, not desperation
Use one of these versions.

Strong version:

> JPL is a particularly meaningful environment for me because it offers the chance to connect the research program I have been building with the scientific needs of two defining cosmology missions. I would approach this role with the focus and commitment of someone who sees Roman and Euclid as the natural place for field-level and forward-modeling methods to become useful at mission scale. I would be excited to contribute not only independent scientific ideas, but also the collaborative, careful, and reproducible work needed to make weak-lensing and clustering measurements decisive tests of cosmic growth, gravity, and dark energy.

More conservative version:

> JPL is a particularly strong fit for my research because Roman and Euclid require exactly the combination of nonlinear structure modeling, statistical inference, and survey interpretation that has shaped my work. I would be excited to contribute to this mission-driven environment, where methodological rigor is directly connected to the scientific return of major cosmological datasets.
