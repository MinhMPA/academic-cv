# Research statement outline: JPL Roman/Euclid positions

## Suggested title
**Field-level and forward modeling for Roman and Euclid dark-sector cosmology**

## Suggested subtitle
**Extracting robust growth, gravity, and dark-energy information from weak lensing and galaxy clustering**

## Length and tone
Target 3-4 pages unless the application instructions require otherwise.

Tone:

- Mission-oriented, not purely faculty-style.
- Ambitious but not inflated.
- Technically credible to cosmologists.
- Clear enough for a broad astrophysics search committee.
- Emphasize deliverables and collaboration.

## Opening: research vision
Suggested opening:

> The next generation of space-based cosmology surveys will turn weak lensing and galaxy clustering into precision tests of the dark sector. Roman and Euclid will measure both the geometry of the Universe and the growth of cosmic structure with unprecedented statistical power. The limiting challenge will be whether our modeling, inference methods, and control of systematics can keep pace with the data.
>
> My research addresses this challenge by developing field-level and forward-modeling approaches for large-scale-structure cosmology. The central goal is to extract the full cosmological information content of galaxy surveys while maintaining physical control over nonlinear structure formation, galaxy bias, intrinsic alignments, survey selection, and covariance. At JPL, I would apply this program to Roman and Euclid weak-lensing and galaxy-clustering science, with the aim of producing robust tests of cosmic growth, gravity, dark energy, and possible departures from LambdaCDM.

Programmatic line:

> My long-term goal is to make galaxy surveys into field-level laboratories for fundamental physics.

## Section 2: Proposed JPL research program
Use three to four pillars.

### Pillar A: Joint weak-lensing and galaxy-clustering inference for Roman and Euclid
Motivation:

- Weak lensing maps projected matter.
- Galaxy clustering maps biased tracers.
- Together they constrain geometry, growth, galaxy bias, intrinsic alignments, and gravity.

Possible projects:

- Develop joint modeling frameworks for cosmic shear, galaxy clustering, galaxy-galaxy lensing, and cross-correlations.
- Quantify information overlap and complementarity between lensing and clustering.
- Test gravity/growth consistency between lensing-inferred matter and clustering-inferred tracer fields.
- Build compressed diagnostics that are mission-usable, even if motivated by field-level methods.

Suggested text:

> I will develop joint weak-lensing and clustering inference strategies that treat galaxy bias, matter clustering, intrinsic alignments, and survey selection as coupled modeling problems rather than independent nuisance terms. A central goal will be to identify which combinations of Roman and Euclid observables provide the cleanest tests of growth and gravity, and which modeling assumptions dominate the residual uncertainty.

### Pillar B: Roman-Euclid cross-calibration and consistency
Motivation:

- Roman and Euclid overlap is not just extra area; it is a calibration and validation laboratory.
- Roman depth and precision can help characterize galaxy populations, selection, and systematics relevant to Euclid-wide analyses.

Possible projects:

- Calibrate photometric redshifts and selection functions.
- Cross-check shear calibration, PSF residuals, intrinsic alignment models.
- Use Roman depth to characterize faint galaxy populations relevant to Euclid.
- Develop consistency tests between Roman-deep and Euclid-wide clustering/lensing maps.
- Investigate optimal summary statistics for overlap regions.

Suggested text:

> The Roman-Euclid overlap is not only a way to increase statistical power; it is a calibration laboratory. I will use forward models and simulation-based validation to ask which systematics can be self-calibrated from overlap data, which require external priors, and which propagate most strongly into dark-energy and modified-gravity constraints.

### Pillar C: Field-level and forward-modeling validation of mission likelihoods
Motivation:

- Baseline mission analyses will often use summary statistics.
- Field-level methods can test them, quantify information loss, and guide targeted extensions.

Possible projects:

- Build controlled mock challenges comparing 2pt, 3pt, density-split, field-level, and hybrid analyses.
- Use field-level inference to quantify information beyond standard weak-lensing/clustering summaries.
- Test EFT-informed likelihoods against simulations.
- Define scale cuts and model-error budgets.
- Develop validation datasets and reproducible code.

Suggested text:

> My goal is not to force an all-or-nothing transition to field-level inference. Instead, I will use field-level and forward-modeling tools as a validation and discovery framework: to determine where standard analyses are sufficient, where they fail, and where targeted higher-order or field-level information can materially improve Roman and Euclid cosmology.

### Pillar D: Dark-sector interpretation: growth, gravity, and dark energy
Motivation:

- Cosmic acceleration may reflect dark energy, modified gravity, or more subtle new physics.
- Weak lensing and clustering jointly probe geometry and growth.

Possible projects:

- Growth-index tests.
- E_G-style lensing-clustering consistency tests.
- Gravitational slip / mu-Sigma parameterizations.
- Dark-energy equation-of-state and growth consistency.
- LambdaCDM stress tests.
- Neutrino/dark-sector degeneracies if appropriate.

Suggested text:

> I will use Roman and Euclid weak-lensing and clustering data to construct consistency tests between geometry and growth. These tests are particularly valuable because cosmic acceleration may reflect either a new energy component or a modification of gravity. My previous work on growth suppression and modified-gravity phenomenology provides a natural foundation for interpreting Roman/Euclid measurements in this broader theoretical space.

Suggested closing:

> Roman and Euclid will define the next decade of space-based dark-sector cosmology. Their scientific return will depend not only on the quality of the data, but on the rigor of the modeling and inference frameworks used to interpret weak lensing and galaxy clustering. My research program is designed for this regime: it combines nonlinear large-scale-structure theory, forward modeling, field-level inference, and dark-sector phenomenology. At JPL, I would use this background to help turn Roman and Euclid data into robust, high-precision tests of growth, gravity, and dark energy, while contributing to the collaborative mission environment needed to make these measurements scientifically durable.
