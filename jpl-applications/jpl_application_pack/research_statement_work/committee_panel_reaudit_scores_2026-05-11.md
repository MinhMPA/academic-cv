They now have a clearer technical identity, a more concrete Roman--Euclid validation program, and better defensive language around field-level inference as a validation framework rather than a proposed replacement for mission-standard analyses. A further revision added three important counters to the prior weak spots: responsibility for the PFS full-shape galaxy-clustering analysis pipeline, named scientific-leadership roles, and mentorship of students or early-career researchers whose projects led to papers or accepted workshop publications.

The Scientist II statement remains the cleaner match. It reads like a high-impact contributor with an independent technical niche, and the ambition level is credible for the role. The Scientist IV statement is now significantly less exposed than before: the PFS pipeline responsibility gives it real survey-analysis ownership, while the workshop chair, COSMO'24 convener role, and publication-producing mentorship strengthen the leadership case. The remaining IV risk is narrower: the evidence is still not Roman/Euclid-internal mission leadership, but it is now much more than adjacent promise.
