## Roman and Euclid specifics

- Roman and Euclid are complementary dark-energy missions. Euclid covers a wider area; Roman is deeper and higher precision over a smaller area. Their overlap motivates cross-calibration and joint inference rather than two separate mission narratives.
- NASA/JPL context emphasizes that both missions will map structure and use weak lensing plus galaxy clustering to test dark energy and gravity. This supports making "geometry and growth" a core phrase in the statements.
- NASA Roman material on weak lensing stresses 3D matter mapping, dark matter, dark energy, and complementarity with other probes. This supports linking field-level and forward modeling to validation of matter maps, distance estimates, and systematic corrections.
- Euclid material emphasizes weak lensing and galaxy clustering as primary cosmological probes, with large galaxy samples and growth/expansion measurements. This supports the proposed joint lensing-clustering program.

The About section presents Minh as a cosmologist at the interface of nonlinear structure formation, statistical inference, and galaxy surveys, with forward-modeling and field-level Bayesian inference used to test growth, gravity, dark energy, and primordial physics.

Safe application wording: "I contributed to evidence for late-time suppression in the growth of large-scale structure" or "my first-author work found evidence for suppressed late-time growth."
