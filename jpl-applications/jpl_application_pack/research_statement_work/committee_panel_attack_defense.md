# Committee Panel Attack and Defense Review

Date: 2026-05-11

Scope:

- `jpl-applications/jpl-research-mentoring-statement/research_statement_R5541_Scientist_IV.tex`
- `jpl-applications/jpl-research-mentoring-statement/research_statement_R5540_Scientist_II.tex`

Method:

- `paper-audit` deep-review scripts were run as mechanical context. They found no critical issues or submission blockers, but their main alerts were paper-genre false positives for an application statement, especially missing abstract and long technical sentences.
- `cosmo-paper-reviewer` was applied manually as a four-person cosmology/application panel: science-case editor, Roman/Euclid survey-systematics reviewer, theory/modeling reviewer, and statistics/failure-mode reviewer.
- Each row first attacks the statement as a skeptical committee member, then reverses stance and defends the statement/application.

## Executive Verdict

The statements are scientifically credible and strategically coherent, but they can still be shot down on three fronts: they do not yet name a sufficiently concrete Roman/Euclid deliverable, they lean on field-level inference as the unifying identity without fully proving mission immediacy, and the Scientist IV version asks the reader to infer senior mission leadership readiness from adjacent evidence rather than from direct Roman/Euclid leadership. The defense is also real: the drafts are appropriately scoped for a short application statement, avoid reckless claims, and make a strong bridge from growth anomalies, field-level inference, intrinsic alignment, and multi-survey calibration to exactly the modeling and validation bottlenecks Roman/Euclid will face.

## Strongest Attack

The harshest committee line would be: "This is an excellent LSS/field-level inference researcher trying to enter Roman/Euclid cosmology, but the statement does not yet prove direct weak-lensing or mission-analysis readiness. For Scientist IV, the leadership language is ahead of the documented mission-specific experience."

## Strongest Defense

The best defense is: "The jobs are not asking for a shear-pipeline engineer alone; they need scientists who can turn Roman/Euclid weak-lensing and clustering data into controlled dark-sector inference. Minh's record in growth tests, field-level likelihoods, intrinsic alignment, and multi-survey calibration is unusually well matched to the modeling, validation, and interpretation bottlenecks. The statements avoid claiming prior Roman/Euclid leadership and instead propose a credible path into mission-relevant validation work."
